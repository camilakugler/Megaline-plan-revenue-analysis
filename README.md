# Megaline Prepaid Plan Revenue Analysis

## Overview

In this project, I analyzed the behavior of 500 Megaline customers who used one of two prepaid plans, **Surf** or **Ultimate**, during 2018.

My goal was to understand how customers use calls, messages, and mobile data, calculate the monthly revenue generated by each plan, and determine whether the difference in average revenue is statistically significant.

## Business Question

Megaline's commercial team wanted to know which prepaid plan generates more revenue so the company could make better decisions about its advertising budget.

I focused on two main questions:

1. Does the average monthly revenue differ between Surf and Ultimate?
2. Does the average monthly revenue of customers in the NY-NJ area differ from customers in other regions?

## Tools

`Python` `Pandas` `NumPy` `Matplotlib` `SciPy` `Jupyter Notebook` `EDA` `Statistical Analysis` `Hypothesis Testing`

## Dataset

I worked with customer, call, message, internet usage, and plan data from **500 Megaline customers in 2018**.

The data includes customer profiles, plan subscriptions, call duration, text-message activity, mobile internet usage, monthly plan fees, included allowances, and overage charges.

## Analysis Process

I prepared the data by converting date fields, inspecting missing values, and aggregating customer activity by month. I then combined usage information with customer and plan data to calculate monthly revenue for each customer.

After preparing the analytical dataset, I compared:

- call duration by plan;
- message activity by plan;
- internet usage by plan;
- monthly revenue distributions;
- revenue variability between the plans;
- average revenue using statistical hypothesis tests.

## Key Findings

I found that call behavior was very similar between the two plans. Surf customers averaged approximately **404.76 minutes per month**, while Ultimate customers averaged approximately **406.19 minutes**.

Ultimate customers sent more messages on average: approximately **37.55 messages per month**, compared with **31.16 for Surf**.

Internet consumption was also similar, at approximately **16.17 GB for Surf** and **16.81 GB for Ultimate**.

The clearest difference appeared in revenue:

| Plan | Average Monthly Revenue |
|---|---:|
| Surf | **$57.29** |
| Ultimate | **$72.12** |

Surf showed much greater revenue variability because customers exceeded the plan allowances more frequently, while Ultimate revenue was more concentrated around its higher monthly fee.

## Hypothesis Testing

### Surf vs. Ultimate

I tested:

- **H₀:** the average monthly revenue is the same for Surf and Ultimate.
- **H₁:** the average monthly revenue is different.

Using Welch's independent two-sample t-test with **α = 0.05**, I obtained a p-value of approximately **4.88 × 10⁻²⁵**.

I rejected the null hypothesis and concluded that the difference in average monthly revenue between the plans is statistically significant.

### NY-NJ vs. Other Regions

I also tested whether average monthly revenue differs between NY-NJ customers and customers from other regions.

The p-value was approximately **0.0186**, so I rejected the null hypothesis at the 5% significance level.

## Conclusion

Based on average monthly revenue, I found that **Ultimate is the stronger plan from a revenue-per-customer perspective**, generating approximately **$72.12 per customer-month compared with $57.29 for Surf**.

At the same time, Surf users exceeded their allowances more frequently, creating greater variability in monthly charges. I see this as a potential business opportunity: Megaline could investigate whether an intermediate plan would better serve customers whose usage consistently falls between Surf and Ultimate.
